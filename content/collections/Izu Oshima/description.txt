I arrived on a cold and rainy day and after debarking the ferry which slid in between walls of tetrapods and filling my stomach with perhaps the best bowl of Kaisendon I've had in my life. I explored the sleepy fishing town of Okata. walked past docked fishing boats, houses with paint peeled by the salty sea air and taking a moment to take in the quiet town shrine. From there with the pushed our small kei van to the limit to climb up, passing through fields of Japanese Silver Grass to the lip of Mt. Mihara, the active volcano that in 1986 caused an island wide evacuation in it's eruption which deposited the very same lava flows I hiked across.


Climbing to the top the weather started to break, and the sun started poking through the clouds, illuminating the fields of silvergrass growing amongst the durable lava flows, on the other side of the peak, the black sand Ura-sabaku Desert opened up to us exposing an almost lunar landscape. From there we were lucky enough to stay in a small fishing village on the southern side of the island and were delighted to watch the fisherman go out to sea for their daily catch before making our journey back to Tokyo.
